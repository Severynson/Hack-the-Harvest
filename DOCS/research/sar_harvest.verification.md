## Corrected claims

- **sar_harvest-1**: For EU maize (LUCAS class B16, grain and silage not separated; 541 polygons, 2018), the Sentinel-1 VH/VV end-of-season date comes on average 18.6 days after the NDVI end-of-season date (mean absolute difference 25.5 days, r = 0.52). In Germany (65-68 polygons), VH/VV end of season matched recorded harvest (BBCH 99, 1.2 days later) and NDVI matched early ripening (BBCH 83, 6.5 days earlier). The paper does not report grain maize separately.
- **sar_harvest-2**: In Dutch maize (335 parcels, 2017, late August to mid-September) VH stayed flat near -16.2 dB while VV rose 2 dB, so VH/VV fell about 2 dB; in Austria VH/VV tracked corn plant water (R2 = 0.87, two field-seasons). A pre-harvest coherence rise is reported for spring wheat in Kazakhstan, not corn. That a VH/VV or coherence change-point fires early on grain corn, and VH does not, is a hypothesis to test on labeled fields.
- **sar_harvest-3**: No opened source measures the corn harvest step. Upper bounds inferred from seasonal range are about 6 dB in VH/VV (Austria, -12 to -6 dB during green-up) and about 7 dB in VH (Netherlands, leaf development). In the wet Dutch autumn of 2017 the VH drop was not observable, and winter bare-soil VH (-12 to -15 dB) sat at or above the canopy plateau.
- **sar_harvest-4**: Sentinel-1 sampling over US corn country is uneven and often thinner than 6 days: central Iowa, east-central Wisconsin and most Corn Belt points get one satellite per track at 12 days, mostly evening passes. Six-day repeats exist in California's Central Valley and in a north-central Iowa band (about 42.6-43.2N at -93.6: 32 looks in fall 2026, 33 in fall 2025). Check each study county.
- **sar_harvest-5**: Published Sentinel-1 harvest detectors reach date errors of about 6 to 13 days (MAE 6.5 days on 12-day data in Kazakhstan; RMSE 11.4 to 13.3 days for maize in Serbia on 6-day data) and detection rates of 56 to 85 percent. None of the opened studies is from the US Corn Belt, and none separates silage from grain.

## Missed or backwards

1. A 6-day Sentinel-1 band crosses north-central Iowa in both seasons (Sentinel-1A + 1C in 2025, 1C + 1D in 2026), about 90 km north of the researcher's test point. Central Iowa also switches satellite between seasons (1A in 2025, 1D in 2026). ASF query above.
2. Meroni's maize class cannot be called grain maize. LUCAS B16 is "areas where maize (Zea mays) is planted", and the paper describes maize as "harvested when still relatively green". The 18.6-day gap may partly reflect silage. https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/205002/8072634/LUCAS2018-C3-Classification.pdf
3. Khabbazan Fig. 7: after the 2017 maize harvest the mean VH stayed near -15 dB and rose in winter, while spring bare soil was near -24 dB. A fixed -19 dB "bare" threshold fails on wet soil. The instrumented Dutch parcel was silage maize. https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-11-01887/article_deploy/remotesensing-11-01887.pdf
4. Denser sampling did not fix maize date error: Mimic et al. had data "every six days at a minimum" from both orbit directions and still reported maize RMSE 11.4-13.3 days and R2 0.46-0.48. https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=11990955&rettype=xml

## Overall

No fabricated sources: every citation exists and every quoted number was found in the source. The weaknesses are in wording and generalization: "grain maize" (source is all maize), "only California" (false for northern Iowa), "12-day data" (one study used 6-day), and dB harvest-step ranges that are inferred, not measured. The archive and catalog counts are reproducible today.
