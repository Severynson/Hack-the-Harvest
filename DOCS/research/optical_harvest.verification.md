## Corrected claims

- **optical_harvest-1:** A peer-reviewed, transparent optical classifier for silage versus grain maize exists (Lin et al. 2025, Hohhot, Inner Mongolia): a three-condition decision tree on Whittaker-smoothed Sentinel-2 NDVI and LSWI plus an elevation mask, whose silage/grain split is a single rule, September LSWI <= 0.15. Reported OA 0.9291 versus 0.9043 for a random forest, but the two were not scored on the same samples (tree: all 381 points after threshold tuning; RF: 30% hold-out of about 115 points), so "beat a random forest" is not established. Thresholds were manually re-tuned for 2022 (OA 0.8621) and 2021 (OA 0.8816).
- **optical_harvest-2:** A NIR-SWIR moisture index (NDMI = LSWI = NDII) is a reasonable lead signal: LSWI had the highest Gini importance in Lin et al.'s three-class random forest (silage / grain / non-maize), and in Zhou et al. 2022 field spectrometry NDII tracked maize canopy water content better than NDVI (R2 0.79 versus 0.69). It is not a clean win: NDII saturates at the same vegetation water content as NDVI (0.55 kg m-2), and Zhou et al. conclude that WI and NDWI (1240 nm, not on Sentinel-2 or Landsat) reflect crop water best.
- **optical_harvest-4:** At operational scale with HLS v2.0, the WIST algorithm detected 94% of cover-crop terminations on 28,190 enrolled Maryland fields (2020-2021), agreeing with farmer-reported dates within one week for about 49%, two weeks for 72%, three for 84% and four for 90%. Treat week-level timing as the demonstrated operational performance against farmer-reported (imperfect) dates, not a proven ceiling. Do not cite the "field verification time cut by at least 50%" figure without a source.
- **optical_harvest-6:** NOAA mean clear days differ by roughly a factor of two to three between California's Central Valley and the Upper Midwest in August to October (Fresno 26/24/20 versus Madison 9/9/9), less in November (12 versus 5). The interval widths are our own estimate, not a published result: with HLS (1.6-day mean revisit, 1.4 days in 2025) about 2 days in California and about 4-7 days in the Upper Midwest in September-October; "one to three weeks" applies to Sentinel-2 alone. NOAA "clear days" are daytime-average sky cover of 0-3 tenths from a legacy record (46-57 years of record), not clear-sky probability at overpass time.
- **optical_harvest-7:** When grain corn stands in the field after maturity, harvest falls on a vegetation-index plateau and is hard to date from NDVI-curve features; a normalised NIR/NDVI ratio (NHPI, threshold 0.6 within two months after mid-senescence) dated corn harvest with MAE 6.43 days (R2 0.749, N = 151) at 500 m MODIS scale in a 2025 preprint. The Santana et al. 2025 double-logistic numbers (RMSE 6-15 days, +3 to +16 day offsets) were not verified and should not be presented until the source is in hand.

## Things the research missed or got backwards

1. **Lin et al.'s headline accuracy is in-sample for the decision tree.** 354/381 = 0.9291 reproduces the reported OA exactly, so the tree was scored on every sample point after "iterative threshold optimization"; the RF was scored on a 30% hold-out. https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/agronomy/agronomy-15-01438/article_deploy/agronomy-15-01438.pdf
2. **Drop detectors confuse other biomass removals with the target event.** In De Vroey et al.'s independent field campaign the detector reached F1 58% and precision 44% on all permanent grasslands; almost half of false positives overlapped grazing, and 54% of false positives came from Sentinel-1 alone. The 79% applies only after grazed parcels are dropped. https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/handle/2078.5/260919
3. **HLS revisit is now better than the figure used.** NASA Earthdata states 1.6 days in 2022 with four satellites and 1.4 days in 2025 with five; the Upper Midwest interval estimate should be redone with this and should not be presented as one to three weeks. https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/hls
4. **NOAA "clear days" is a pessimistic proxy for usable observations.** It is the count of days with daytime-average sky cover of 0-3 tenths; partly cloudy days still yield clear pixels. Gao 2020 measured 10.5% clear Sentinel-2 pixel-days at Beltsville, about one clear look every 9-10 days, better than the clear-day arithmetic predicts. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/comparative-climatic-data
