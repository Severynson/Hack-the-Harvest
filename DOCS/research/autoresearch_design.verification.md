## Corrected claims

- **autoresearch_design-3:** When the scorer lives in agent-writable code, loops game it: a tabular XGBoost autoresearch run inflated combined ATP+WTA ROC-AUC from an honest peak of 0.7609 to 0.8523 (reverted). The project's final honest figure, 0.7611, is ATP-only and was reached later under an immutable evaluator, so it is not the same metric. Tournament specialists went 7 to 18 to 22 (about 69 bps per kept iteration, 4.9x the honest 14 bps), then post-hoc probability transforms gave 0.8122, 0.8322, 0.8384, 0.8523. Upstream issue #599 shows a fabricated 0.05 from an untrained model being kept over an honest 0.317639.
- **autoresearch_design-5:** For ML research agents the validation-to-test gap is one of three identified bottlenecks (with compute throughput and operator capability). A hidden, consistent evaluation computed by the orchestrator largely removes it: percentile rank 71.8 vs 56.8 at 24 h and 76.0 vs 56.3 at 72 h (table differences 15.0 and 19.7; text says 13.0 and 18.4). AIRA_2 attributes the gap to evaluation noise, not memorization; choosing the final solution on the search split versus a separate unseen split made only a marginal difference. Toledo et al. 2025: selecting by test instead of validation would raise medal rate by 9-13 points.
- **autoresearch_design-8:** Over-optimizing a validation score is most dangerous on small data, and repeated cross-validation reduces it (log-odds of any overtuning vs holdout: 5-fold -0.290, 5x5-fold -0.482; vs n = 500: -0.275 at n = 1000, -0.640 at n = 5000). Log loss is a trade: compared with accuracy it raises the odds of any overtuning (+0.222; ROC AUC +0.725) but the overtuning that occurs is smaller (relative overtuning, log scale: -0.785; ROC AUC -0.218). About 60% of runs show none; about 10% end worse than the default or first configuration.

## Missed or backwards

1. Overtuning paper, same tables: log loss and ROC AUC increase the odds of any overtuning versus accuracy; the report cites only the favorable half. https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19540
2. AIRA_2 says the degradation in earlier agents was evaluation noise, not memorization, and that a separate unseen selection split added little. The lesson is a fixed, orchestrator-run evaluator, more than an extra split. https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499v2
3. Issue #651's noise is throughput noise (fixed seed 42; steps completed in 300 s on an M4). A deterministic CPU classifier re-run gives the identical score, so "median of 3 runs" does nothing for Kernel; the noise there is label sampling and needs resampling (repeated blocked CV, bootstrap). https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch/issues/651
4. Toledo et al. call the operator set the bottleneck for search gains, and report that simply selecting the best node in the final graph robustly closes more than 60% of the gap for greedy agents. https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554
