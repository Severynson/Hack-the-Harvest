## Corrected claims

**dairy_prior-1.** A public California Water Boards file lists active dairies with coordinates and a population field, current to 3 November 2026: 1,063 active "Mature dairy cattle" facilities, 1,061 with coordinates, 950 with `cafo_population` summing to 1,447,903 head (85% of NASS's 1,710,000 milk cows). It is the most current source but not the only one with both. CARB's California Dairy and Livestock Database (CADD v2.0.0, October 2025) gives latitude and longitude for 2,115 facilities and yearly herd by class for 2012 to 2023 (2023: 1,032 dairies, 1,350,194 milk cows plus 204,925 dry cows). CARB's technical document says the CIWQS population field is "primarily used for billing purposes" and does not match the herd sizes in annual reports.

**dairy_prior-3.** In California's dairy counties the dairy prior carries almost no information: across the 10 counties with all values disclosed in the 2022 Census, silage is 50% to 99% of corn acres (median 0.97), R squared is 0.07 against cows per corn acre, and the mass-balance rule (error 0.160) does worse than the state average (0.098). Statewide, 2022 had 483,437 silage acres against 34,512 grain acres (93% silage). This does not hold everywhere or every year. Low-dairy Sacramento Valley and Delta counties left out of the 10 grow mostly grain (Sutter 412 grain acres and no silage, Colusa 338 and none, Butte 1,594, Yolo 2,486, Solano 38% grain). Grain was 94,541 acres in 2017 (81% silage) and is 75,000 acres in NASS's revised 2025 figures (83% silage).

## Missed or backwards

1. **A second California source has coordinates and herd size, and it questions the first.** CARB CADD v2.0.0: 2,115 facilities with latitude and longitude, herd by class per year 2012 to 2023, built from annual reports. Its technical document (9 June 2026) says the CIWQS CAFO population is mainly a billing field. https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/california-dairy-livestock-database-cadd (workbook https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/CADD%20v2.0.0.xlsx ; technical document https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/Technical_Documentation_v2.0.0_Final.pdf)
2. **The 2025 revision has a date and a reason.** The researcher wrote that they could not find when or why the figures moved. Grain Stocks, released 30 September 2026, pages 17 to 19, revised 2025 corn acreage, yield and production after the normal end-of-marketing-year balance-sheet review. US corn silage went from 6,208 to 6,187 thousand acres and from 135,540 to 135,090 thousand tons; California is now 450 planted, 75 grain, 370 silage. The January figures in the brief are superseded. https://sb.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/grst0926.pdf
3. **NASS publishes yearly county silage estimates for Wisconsin, and they swing far more than herds do.** "2023 Corn for Silage County Estimates" (23 February 2024) gives 2022 and 2023 by county: Manitowoc 46,300 to 20,600 acres, Barron 16,500 to 1,580, Rock 7,890 to 16,200, state 870,000 to 780,000. A fixed herd-based prior fitted on the 2022 Census cannot follow that. The 2022 survey state total (870,000) is also 10% above the 2022 Census (787,423). https://www.nass.usda.gov/sites/default/files/Statistics_by_State/Wisconsin/Publications/County_Estimates/2024/WI-CtyEst-CornSilage-02-24.pdf
4. **The California "no information" result is partly a sample artefact.** Disclosure rules remove the counties with few cows from the 10-county test, and those are the counties where corn is mostly grain (see the corrected claim). At field level, a low dairy density in the Sacramento Valley is evidence for grain. https://sb.nass.usda.gov/Publications/AgCensus/2022/Full_Report/Volume_1,_Chapter_2_County_Level/California/st06_2_025_025.pdf

## Overall

The numbers can be trusted: every figure I could recompute from the cited files reproduced exactly or within rounding, and no citation was missing or invented. Two claims overreach in wording, not in arithmetic: the Water Boards file is not the only California source with coordinates and herd size, and California corn is not nearly all silage outside the dairy counties or outside 2022. Use NASS's revised 30 September 2026 figures for 2025, and treat the transport bands as inference from seven survey responses.
