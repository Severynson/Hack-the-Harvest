## Corrected claims

**agronomy-5:** California cannot be split by date. In the southern San Joaquin Valley, single-crop silage is planted by early April and chopped in August; double-crop silage follows winter forage, is planted in late May and chopped in September (UC 2012 study; October is not in the source). Sacramento Valley and northern San Joaquin grain corn is planted late March-April, irrigated May through July, dried in the field and combined in September under 14% grain moisture (UC 2015 study). NASS 2025: 420K planted, 365K silage, 50K grain.

**agronomy-6:** NASS enumerator training (December 2023) defines corn for grain as "all corn harvested other than for seed or silage. The end use does not matter", silage as chopping up the entire plant, and "all other purposes" as grazed off, abandoned or any other purpose. Seed corn is asked as a separate question. The slides do not mention high-moisture corn, earlage or snaplage: treating them as grain is an inference from the "other than for seed or silage" wording, not a quoted NASS rule. Planted minus harvested (grain plus silage) is 0.9-3.1% of planted acres for 2000-2025 (mean 1.64%; 1.34%, or 1.32M acres, in 2025).

## Missed by the researcher

1. The 13-day figure is the short end. Purdue (Nielsen) puts half-milkline "2 to 3 weeks prior to kernel blacklayer", and the 200 GDD figure is Purdue's, not Lauer's. Lauer's own summary page calls the milk line only "a guide" and says to measure whole-plant moisture once it starts to move. Present the lead time as 13 days to 3 weeks. https://www.agry.purdue.edu/ext/corn/news/timeless/GrainDrying.html ; https://corn.agronomy.wisc.edu/Management/L031.aspx
2. The 2010 handbook windows look about a week late for Wisconsin silage today. NASS's 5-year average is 68% chopped by 28 Sep (2025 report) and 77% by 4 Oct (2026 report). The handbook's 15-85% window of Sep 13-Oct 13 implies about 50% and 64% on those dates (my linear interpolation). Grain is closer: 43% average by 25 Oct against about 38% implied. https://www.nass.usda.gov/sites/default/files/Statistics_by_State/Wisconsin/Publications/Crop_Progress_&_Condition/2025/WI-Crop-Progress-09-29-25.pdf ; https://www.nass.usda.gov/sites/default/files/Statistics_by_State/Wisconsin/Publications/Crop_Progress_&_Condition/2026/WI-Crop-Progress-10-05-26.pdf ; https://release.nass.usda.gov/reports/prog4326.txt
3. The 2026 season is in the same reports and makes the "early is not silage" point more strongly than 2025. Kansas grain was 18% harvested by 6 Sep 2026 and 40% by 20 Sep 2026 (2025: 2 and 19; 2021-2025 average: 9 and 28). Wisconsin silage was 87% chopped by 4 Oct 2026 with grain at 8%. https://release.nass.usda.gov/reports/prog3826.txt ; https://release.nass.usda.gov/reports/prog3626.txt
4. The national Crop Progress stages are judgment calls, not measurements. NASS defines "mature" as "safe from frost" with "no green foliage present", defines "harvested" generically as "cut, threshed, or otherwise gathered from the field" (no grain or silage distinction), and says interpretations may vary by state. Treat these series as soft validation, not ground truth. https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/National_Crop_Progress/Terms_and_Definitions/index.php

## Overall

Five of the eight findings match their sources number for number, and I recomputed the NASS acreage figures from the original text reports. Two need small wording fixes: the double-crop silage chop month is September in the cited study, and the high-moisture corn rule is an inference that the cited slides do not state. The handbook finding (agronomy-3) is unverified because its host was unreachable from this network, so re-open that file before quoting its dates to USDA.
