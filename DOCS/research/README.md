# Research notes: silage or grain (Kernel, Hack the Harvest 2026)

Compiled 2026-11-14 by cc-daniel. Each topic file has a `.verification.md` beside it: a second agent re-opened the sources. No invented citation was found. Where a topic file and its verification file disagree, the verification file wins. The five `gap_*.md` files were written last and correct several "none found" claims in the topic files.

## Decisions these notes support

1. **Framing.** Kernel is a supplemental harvest-fate indication for NASS's Agricultural Statistics Board: grain, silage and other acres by state. It is not a map and not a replacement for the survey.
2. **Evidence per field.** Cut interval (last seen standing, first seen cut; optical and radar together); wet and green at the cut (NDMI first, NDVI second); size and rate of the drop; dry-down in growing degree days; ground left on dry, bare scenes (NDVI below 0.3); a flag for every data gap. Do not use the cut date relative to the county median.
3. **Decision.** A two-question rule, then a monotone scorecard. XGBoost, TabPFN and MiniRocket are comparison rows. Sigmoid calibration, not isotonic. Outcomes: silage-like, grain-like, inconclusive, not assessable, plus "standing" on the as-of date. High-moisture corn and earlage count as grain.
4. **Acres.** Never sum field labels. Use prior-corrected state shares (EM), an adjusted-count interval, three buckets and a ratio estimate on planted acres. Report the independent rollup and the NASS-benchmarked rollup separately; quote accuracy only for the first.
5. **Baselines to beat.** Always grain; same as last year (12 to 15% state error on silage acres); the FSA intended-use ratio; the Lin et al. 2025 moisture rule.
6. **Corn mask.** CDL 2025 classes 1, 225, 226, 228, 237 and 241 (flag 12 and 13). No 2026 CDL exists before early 2027.
7. **New data sources.** Kern, Stanislaus and Yolo county permit polygons (declared corn use); Quebec BDPPAD parcels; FSA county acres by intended use (10 Nov 2026); RMA insured type; Wisconsin weekly silage progress.
8. **Experiment loop.** Lock 25 to 30% of labeled fields by whole county; pin `prepare.py` by hash; accept only gains of 2.5 standard errors; cap the search at about 150 scored experiments.
9. **Agents (Nemotron).** Typed, tested tools only; no model-written code or numbers. The Index Scout logs each candidate with its expected sign before scoring.
10. **Limits.** Earth Engine allows 150 EECU-hours a month (pilot 200 fields first). HLS runs two to three weeks behind (use it for the 2025 replay only). Sentinel-1 revisits every 12 days per track outside California and north-central Iowa.

Still open: which year and states the labeled fields cover, how many there are, and how "silage-like" was assigned.

## Index

**Corrections that override the headlines below**

- The Lin et al. 2025 rule did not fairly beat a random forest: the rule was scored in-sample on 381 points, the forest on a 30% hold-out.
- Public US field-level declarations do exist: Kern, Stanislaus and Yolo county permit polygons (`gap_us_field_labels_california.md`).
- FSA's county file does split corn by intended use (2.89M silage acres for 2026). It is wider than RMA's but covers only 47% of the silage NASS later reports, and it never beat "same as last year" (`gap_fsa_ratio_baseline_2026.md`).
- Sentinel-1 gives 6-day repeats in California's Central Valley and in north-central Iowa, not only California. No source measures the corn harvest step in dB, and the 56% radar result came from fields with no corn.
- With HLS, optical cut intervals are a few days wide in the Upper Midwest too. Neither sensor alone should date the cut.
- In Earth Engine the CDL and AlphaEarth embeddings end at 2024: load CDL 2025 from NASS and drop the embedding ablation for 2025.
- Use NASS's revised 2025 figures (30 Sep 2026): 98.713M planted, 90.948M grain, 6.187M silage; California 450K planted, 75K grain, 370K silage.
- A 2026 in-season crop map exists at George Mason University, tested only in Nebraska and Iowa (`gap_corn_mask_2026.md`).

### The signal

- [`precedent.md`](precedent.md): Direct precedent: studies that separate silage maize from grain maize by remote sensing. Direct precedent is thin. One dedicated study (Lin 2025, Inner Mongolia) separates silage from grain maize with a fixed September Sentinel-2 moisture threshold: silage F1 0.93 in-sample, 0.82-0.89 in other years after hand re-tuning. Germany's national map found the split highly uncertain. No precedent was found for radar, dry-down days, residue, GDD timing or dairy context, nor any US study or agency product (search budget ran out early). Keep the four numbers; lead with NDMI; benchmark the published rule. *(8 findings re-checked: 7 confirmed, 1 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`optical_harvest.md`](optical_harvest.md): Optical time series: detecting the harvest date and the shape of the decline (cliff versus fade). Optical data can separate silage from grain, but it yields an interval, not a date. Copy USDA-ARS's WIST logic (steepest drop between adjacent raw clear looks; midpoint plus or minus half the gap) and lead with NDMI/LSWI, not NDVI: the one published silage-versus-grain rule (September LSWI <= 0.15) hit 93% accuracy. Intervals are days wide in California, one to three weeks in the Upper Midwest, so radar must date the cut. Skip BFAST, CCDC and curve-fit end-of-season dates. *(8 findings re-checked: 3 confirmed, 5 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`sar_harvest.md`](sar_harvest.md): Radar: Sentinel-1 and other public SAR for corn harvest detection and post-harvest field state. Keep radar, but narrow its job. Sentinel-1 VH drops at the cut, not at browning (EU grain maize: radar end of season 18.6 days after NDVI's), so 'dry-down days' is literature-backed. But Corn Belt fields get one satellite at 12-day repeat per track, the harvest step is only a few dB, and wet soil can hide it. Use VH for a cut-date bracket, VH/VV fade for greenness, optical for residue. Extracting 10,000 fields in Earth Engine should be feasible tonight. *(8 findings re-checked: 3 confirmed, 5 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`residue.md`](residue.md): Ground left behind: crop residue cover, bare soil and tillage from Sentinel-2 / Landsat. Residue is a real but fragile harvest-type signal. Silage leaves about 5-15% cover (13-27% with 6-12 inch stubble) versus 75-95% after grain: roughly 0.10 NDTI on dry soil. Wet soil (+0.02 to +0.11), regrowth and fall tillage produce shifts as large. Build it relative, gated (NDVI below 0.3, dry, first scene after the cut), allow missing, and use it as the 'early is not silage' tie-breaker. NASA EMIT's 60 m NPV fraction is a new public cross-check. *(8 findings re-checked: 7 confirmed, 1 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`agronomy.md`](agronomy.md): Agronomy ground truth: regional calendars, dry-down physics and the confusers. The silage/grain difference is thermal, not calendar: silage is cut about 200 GDD before black layer, dry grain 150-375 GDD after, so calendar thresholds fail in the South, Kansas and California. Keep dry-down and residue as lead evidence, add wet-at-cut, post-harvest green-up and an explicit 'other' class, and validate cut-date distributions against NASS weekly silage progress (Wisconsin 66% chopped on 28 Sep 2025). Switchers (2022: grain 3.2M acres below June forecast) are the pitch. *(8 findings re-checked: 5 confirmed, 2 corrected, 1 not verifiable.)*

### Data, statistics and models

- [`labels.md`](labels.md): Public labels and statistical proxies: where silage-versus-grain truth actually exists. No public US parcel-level silage/grain label exists, but Quebec's BDPPAD gives about 50,000 declared grain-versus-forage maize parcels a year, refreshed 9 Nov 2026 for the 2026 season. For county priors, USDA RMA's crop-year-2026 file already splits insured corn into Grain and Silage types (1.24M silage acres, 481 counties), a floor not a total. Wisconsin publishes weekly silage harvest progress by district for timing validation. Use 2026: the 2025 October reports were cancelled. *(8 findings re-checked: 6 confirmed, 2 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`statistics.md`](statistics.md): Statistics: from an imperfect field classifier and few labels to defensible acreage with uncertainty. Do not sum hard labels: with 90% recall and 5% false positives, counting over-states US silage by about two thirds and under-states California by 9%. Build three numbers: a per-state share from prior-shift EM, a label-based interval from adjusted counting (wide with 150 labels), and a NASS-style backtest reliability table against persistence. Replace county-median-relative cut dates and isotonic calibration. NASS weekly silage progress curves give a new label-free cross-check. *(8 findings re-checked: 7 confirmed, 1 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`models.md`](models.md): Model choice: interpretable models, small-label time-series classifiers and foundation embeddings. Build a two-question rule plus a monotone logistic scorecard (or EBM) on the four physical features; published precedent shows threshold rules beating random forest on silage vs grain maize. Treat XGBoost, TabPFN, MiniRocket and one annual AlphaEarth or TESSERA embedding as GPU ablations that test whether the simple model suffices. Swap isotonic for sigmoid calibration, evaluate with leave-one-state-out folds, and roll up acres with the adjusted count, not classify-and-count. *(8 findings re-checked: 6 confirmed, 2 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`data_access.md`](data_access.md): Data access and tooling: getting per-field time series for 10^4-10^5 polygons in hours. Use Earth Engine as the fast path (chunked reduceRegions to pandas); it is current for everything except CDL and AlphaEarth, which both stop at 2024. The real limit is the new 150 EECU-hour monthly quota, so pilot first and sample fields for state rollups. Sentinel-1 revisit ranges from 1-5 days in California to a 24-day hole in eastern Wisconsin, so the cut date must be sensor-agnostic. WebSearch was unavailable; all facts come from direct primary-source fetches. *(8 findings re-checked: 5 confirmed, 3 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*
- [`nowcast.md`](nowcast.md): Nowcasting: the current-year corn mask, earliness versus accuracy, and honest real-time simulation. No 2026 CDL exists (CDL 2025 came Feb 27, 2026) and official 2026 silage acres arrive about Jan 12, 2027. Silage chopping is about 90% done by mid-October in Wisconsin and Minnesota, late October in New York, yet 19% of grain corn was still standing on Nov 8, 2026. Build a dated as-of replay of 2025 with a 'standing' class and a NASS-style reliability table. Caveat: web search was unavailable; evidence comes from directly opened primary pages. *(8 findings re-checked: 5 confirmed, 3 corrected, 0 not verifiable.)*

- [`dairy_prior.md`](dairy_prior.md): Dairies and feedlots: location data, haul distance and a feed-demand mass balance. ### Gap searches (run last; they correct earlier "none found" claims)

- [`gap_us_field_labels_california.md`](gap_us_field_labels_california.md): Public field-level silage/grain declarations exist: Kern (2022-2026) and Stanislaus (2012-2026) publish permit-site polygons with the DPR corn commodity; Yolo stops at 2024. But 2025 is about 99% silage: Kern 751 forage sites vs 5 grain, Stanislaus 1,392 vs 9. Use them tonight as a one-class California silage and double-crop validation set (Yolo 2024 adds 75 grain fields), not as balanced training labels; declarations are forward-looking, sticky and sometimes generic.
- [`gap_corn_mask_2026.md`](gap_corn_mask_2026.md): A 2026 in-season US crop map exists: George Mason's ICDL lists June, July and August 2026 layers, downloadable via free CropSmart sign-in (download untested). August corn F1 was 0.93-0.99 in Nebraska and Iowa only; no dairy-state validation. NASS has no 2026 map. Better still, FSA's November 10 file gives 2026 corn acres by county and intended use (95.2M acres). Use the ICDL as mask, measure it on 2025, and roll up as a ratio on FSA acres.
- [`gap_fsa_ratio_baseline_2026.md`](gap_fsa_ratio_baseline_2026.md): No. A per-state FSA intended-use ratio is worse than "same as last year" in all nine years 2017-2025 (state WAPE 14.4% vs 11.8% in 2024, 15.4% vs 15.1% in 2025) and got Texas backwards twice. FSA covers 47% of NASS silage and 12% or less in KS/NE/IA/SD, where the 2026 drought sits. Public November numbers bracket 2026 at 6.15-6.8M acres; pitch Kernel as the harvest-fate check that resolves that gap.
- [`gap_us_precedent_search.md`](gap_us_precedent_search.md): No US study, thesis or agency paper separating silage from grain corn by satellite was found in 45 searches; the only US-site precedent stays Salehi Shahrabi 2020 (Tulare, silage versus other crops, kappa 0.8). Qualify "first US test". New: Kern County, California publishes field polygons labelled corn forage/fodder versus corn grain (751 versus 5 in 2025) plus winter-forage polygons, a ready test of the double-crop trap. Italy codes silage separately; Portugal, Spain and Catalonia public layers do not.
- [`gap_dairy_feedlot_points.md`](gap_dairy_feedlot_points.md): Facility points with herd size exist outside California. Minnesota's MPCA registry (38,374 points, per-class head counts), Michigan EGLE (272 CAFOs plus 21,301 manured-field polygons), Iowa (10,690) and Climate TRACE's free national API (48,331 US dairies and feedlots) make the tie-breaker a field-level feature tonight. New York and Pennsylvania have points but no usable herd size. Cite haul limits: 'within a few miles' (NDSU 2025), 20 miles (Missouri 2024), 18 km (Manzone 2020).

### Contradictions between topics (resolve before building)

- Which sensor dates the cut: optical_harvest says radar must, because optical gives only an interval. sar_harvest, data_access and markers_ops point the other way: most Corn Belt fields get one 12-day radar track (eastern Wisconsin had a 24-day hole in fall 2025), published radar date errors are 6-13 days, Sen4CAP ranks optical above radar, and the optical fact-check puts HLS gaps at 4-7 days. The build should date the cut optical-first and use radar to fill cloud gaps.
- Sentinel-1 revisit over Iowa is given as 12 days (sar_harvest, per track) and as 5-7 days (data_access, all tracks combined, fall 2025). The detector runs per track, so 12 days sets the cut-date bracket unless tracks are pooled.
- optical_harvest builds green-at-cut on HLS, while data_access and nowcast show HLS stops on 2026-11-05 upstream (25 October over central Iowa) and say to drop it from 2026 in-season evidence. HLS suits a 2025 replay; 2026 needs Sentinel-2 L2A and Landsat directly.
- Which season gets the compute: labels says validate timing on 2026 because the October-November 2025 Crop Progress reports were never published; nowcast, statistics and nass_ops build their replay and reliability table on 2025 or 2023-2025; models assumes 2025 labels; the USDA briefing makes 2026 the headline. The 150 EECU-hour Earth Engine quota will not cover both seasons at scale.
- models recommends an AlphaEarth embedding ablation for a 2025 label year; data_access confirms Earth Engine's AlphaEarth layer ends with 2024.
- The models summary and one residue finding say a published threshold rule beat a random forest on silage versus grain. The precedent and optical fact-checks show that comparison was in-sample (381 points) against a 30% hold-out and within noise. models also calls the Lin et al. thresholds unverified, although two other fact-checkers read the full text.
- Cut date relative to the county median: plan v2 and agronomy keep it; statistics and nowcast say drop it (mid-season it is biased toward silage dates); markers_ops replaces it with an 8 km neighbourhood of up to 100 corn fields.
- Dry-down days: agronomy wants it as the first question of the tree; precedent wants it binned and presented as new and noisy; precedent and optical_harvest lead with NDMI green-at-cut instead. Three definitions are in play (optical browning to radar cut; cut minus mid-senescence; radar cut minus 50% NDVI decline), and the 18.6-day Meroni figure cited for it mixes silage and grain maize.
- Residue: agronomy treats it as lead evidence read within 14 days of the cut; the residue topic calls it fragile, allows 30 days and restricts it to a third question for early cuts.
- 2025 NASS figures appear in two vintages: January 2026 (US silage 6,208 thousand acres; California 420 planted, 50 grain, 365 silage; persistence error 937 thousand acres, 15.1%) and the revision (6,187; California 450, 75, 370; 864 thousand, 14.0%). nowcast names the January vintage as the target, statistics says present the revised one, and the revision is dated 30 September 2026 (dairy_prior, stakeholders), October 2026 (nass_ops) and November 2026 (statistics).
- California's silage share is quoted as 82%, 83%, 87%, 88% and 93% across topics, and the 'always grain' baseline as 92.4% and 94%; the denominators, vintages and years differ and none is stated consistently.
- nass_ops, deployment and markers_ops say NASS survey precision is worst in dairy or silage-heavy states. The nass_ops fact-check shows the large CVs sit in states with tiny grain acreage (California, Arizona, Idaho), while Wisconsin, South Dakota and New York are at 1.1-5.0%.
- agronomy and dairy_prior call state silage acres stable in dairy states, yet Wisconsin rose 21% (730 to 885 thousand acres) from 2024 to 2025, outside the 10-20% reconciliation tolerance agronomy proposes for it.
- The pitch line 'nothing shows silage before January' (nass_ops, stakeholders, deployment) conflicts with the labels fact-check: FSA and RMA already publish in-season county corn silage acres. Four topics still list that FSA question as open; it is closed.
- County prior: labels proposes RMA silage-type acres, its fact-check says FSA is 2.3 times wider, and dairy_prior proposes milk cows per corn acre. One must be chosen, or all three compared in the ablation.
- Class schema: agronomy, nass_ops and stakeholders want three acreage buckets (grain, silage, other); markers_ops wants four decision states (silage-like, grain-like, inconclusive, not assessable); nowcast adds 'standing'. 'Standing means grain' holds in Wisconsin (2.6% silage among standing acres) but not in New York (20%).
- Corn mask classes: stakeholders' recommendation lists 1, 225, 226, 228 and 237; data_access and the stakeholders fact-check add 241.
- sar_harvest says extracting 10,000 fields in Earth Engine tonight is feasible; data_access says the monthly quota is the real limit and nothing should be sized before a 200-field pilot.
- Weekly silage harvest progress: labels and statistics say only Wisconsin is verified; agronomy found Idaho, Oregon and Washington, and nowcast used Minnesota and New York. California is narrative only.
- Evaluation design: statistics locks 30% of labels by county, models uses leave-one-state-out folds, nemotron needs a development split plus a locked holdout, and per-class conformal thresholds need a minimum count per class. A small labeled set cannot satisfy all four.
