## Corrected claim

**residue-5:** A USDA ARS/ERS method already exists for residue cover from Landsat without training data: NDTI is scaled linearly per scene between its minimum and maximum (mean -/+ 3 SD; maximum cover set to 85% for corn and 65% for soybean) after masking clouds, recent rain (NEXRAD 2-day sum > 3 mm) and green cover (NDVI > 0.3), using April-June imagery. Against 200 ARMS survey fields at the 30% line, agreement was 61-67% raw and 68-73% with a +/-5% tolerance. On the 72 fields with four or more usable dates it rose to 78% raw (kappa 0.54) and 85% (kappa 0.66) with the tolerance and crop-specific scaling. Four dates is a quality filter, not a requirement, and R2 against surveyed residue was only 0.09-0.28.

## Missed by the researcher

1. EMIT V002 has a hole exactly where it matters: CMR returns zero V002 granules between 1 Jan 2025 and 30 Jun 2026, and V001 stopped on 31 Aug 2026. Fall 2024 is in both versions, but fall 2025 exists only in V001 and fall 2026 only in V002, so a 2025-to-2026 comparison mixes product versions. The user guide (initial draft, 2 Dec 2025) gives no validation accuracy for the NPV layer. https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?short_name=EMITL2BFRCOV&version=002&temporal=2025-01-01T00:00:00Z,2026-06-30T00:00:00Z ; https://github.com/emit-sds/emit-sds-frcov/blob/develop/docs/EMIT_L2B_Fractional_Cover_User_Guide.md
2. The Beeson method is weaker at field level than the headline suggests, and it is a spring method. It uses April-June scenes (after winter weathering and any fall tillage), was validated on 200 ARMS fields, and explains only 9-28% of the variance in surveyed residue. It also assumes every scene holds both bare and full-residue fields. It has not been tested on a fall, post-harvest window. https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-12-02665/article_deploy/remotesensing-12-02665.pdf
3. NDTI can run backwards on some soils. In Hively 2021's mixing analysis, 7 of 854 soil spectra gave a negative NDTI-residue trend and 22 more a weak one; these soils tended to be rich in kaolinite. Separately, Hively 2018 (citing Gelder 2009) reports that NDVI explains 65% of NDTI model error even below NDVI 0.35, so the 0.3 mask does not remove the green bias. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220003090/downloads/remotesensing-Eval%20of%20SWIR%20Crop.pdf ; https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-10-01657/article_deploy/remotesensing-10-01657.pdf
4. The silage stubble cover figures are estimates, not measurements. Lauer computed them from residue mass with three older mass-to-cover equations, and the ranges are wide: 25-63% at 18 inches and 32-83% at 24 inches. High-chop silage can therefore overlap grain-level cover, which weakens "high residue is strong proof of grain". The article also notes NRCS cover is measured after planting. https://corn.agronomy.wisc.edu/WCM/W105.aspx

## Overall

This topic's findings are well sourced: all eight sources exist and almost every number matches the source text, including the EMIT granule dates and counts, which were reproduced from CMR. One claim (residue-5) overstates the Beeson accuracy by leaving out the tolerance, the 72-field subset and the spring timing. The physical numbers can be presented with confidence; the size of the silage-grain NDTI gap and the timing effects are the researcher's inferences from them, and should be labelled as such.
